# What the game's shaders say each texture slot is

Reproduce with:

    python3 analysis/checks/dump_shader_decls.py analysis/runtime/laptop-port/live-hyst/capture-meta/shaders

605 pixel shaders from an F9 capture (`frame_453279156`, laptop, 2026-10-30). The names come from each
shader's DXBC `RDEF` chunk, so they are the game's own, not an inference from size or format. Read together
with the per-draw bind census in `PROGRESS.md` (286 main-view draws: slot 0 on 283, slot 2 on 285, slot 4 on
139, slot 14 on 286).

## What this settles

* **Slot 0 is the albedo** (`g_ColorTexture`, 297 shaders; `g_Texture0`, 213). The live path takes "the first
  usable PS texture", which lands on slot 0 by luck, not by rule. It should key on the slot.
* **Slot 4 is the normal map** (`g_NormalTexture`, 153 shaders) and the live path never sent it, so every
  surface reached Remix with `roughnessConstant 0.6` and no surface detail. Capturing it is the fix gated by
  `NIER_REMIX_LIVE_NORMAL` (`test_live_normal_map.py`).
* **Slot 17 is ambient occlusion** (`g_AO_Texture`, 146 shaders): unused by us, candidate for later.
* Slots 12 and 19 are fog LUTs (`g_FogLUT`, `g_BeforeFogLUT`, 256 shaders each) and slot 14 is `g_DitherTex`:
  these are bound on nearly every draw and must never be mistaken for a material.
* Nothing in the declarations names a roughness or metalness texture. NieR packs those into the mask
  (`g_MaskTexture`, slot 2) or the colour alpha, which still needs proving channel by channel before we send a
  `roughnessTexture`; until then the constants stay.
